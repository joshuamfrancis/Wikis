# Diet & Workout Plan: Lose Fat, Build Muscle

The approach is body recomposition: a small calorie deficit, high protein, and regular strength training.

## Key Numbers

| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| Daily calories | ~1,900–2,000 kcal |
| Daily protein | ~130 g |
| Daily steps | 7,000–8,000 |
| Strength sessions | 3 per week |
| Expected fat loss | ~0.5 kg per week (2–3 kg per month) |
| Waist target | Under 90 cm |

**Fat loss basics:** 1 kg of body fat takes a deficit of about **7,700 kcal**.

- A 500 kcal workout means about 15 sessions per kg.
- A 300 kcal workout means about 26 sessions per kg.
- It's a net deficit: calories eaten back cancel calories burned.
- Fitness trackers and gym machines often overestimate calories burned by 20–40%.
- Diet changes usually do more of the work than exercise.

- Increase daily steps.
- Add strength training.
- Trim dinner.
- Add protein at breakfast and dinner.

## Easy Lunch Options (Australian Supermarkets)

Each option gives roughly 35–45 g protein and 450–600 kcal.

1. **Roast chicken bowl:** BBQ chicken breast (about 150 g, skin off) + half a brown rice pouch + bagged salad kit. About 5 minutes.
2. **Tuna rice bowl:** a 185 g tin of tuna + half a rice pouch + cucumber, tomato and lemon. Add chilli flakes or pickle for flavour. About 3 minutes.
3. **Egg and chickpea curry:** 3 boiled eggs + half a tin of chickpeas in tikka masala or an onion-tomato masala, with 1 chapati. Batch cook enough for 3 days.
4. **Chicken wrap:** wholemeal wrap + 120 g chicken + lettuce, tomato and Greek yogurt with mint. About 5 minutes.
5. **Salmon and veg tray bake:** salmon + broccoli + baby potatoes with oil spray and spices, 20 minutes at 200°C. Make 2–3 portions at once.
6. **Paneer or tofu bhurji:** 150 g scrambled with onion, tomato and spices, plus 1 chapati or a slice of sourdough. About 10 minutes.
7. **Dal and chicken meal prep:** a big pot of dal + grilled chicken thigh pieces, portioned for the week.

**Rice portions:** half a standard 250 g pouch (e.g. SunRice) is about **⅔ cup cooked**, roughly 125 g and 180–200 kcal. With a 450 g pouch, use about a third.

**Tips:**

- Batch cook on Sunday and Wednesday.
- Keep these on hand: tinned tuna and salmon, eggs, rice pouches, salad kits, frozen vegetables and Greek yogurt.
- Creamy salad-kit dressings can add 150+ kcal. Use half the packet, or swap in lemon and yogurt.

## Easy Dinner Options

Each option gives roughly 30–40 g protein and 500–650 kcal. Keep chapatis to 2–3 (or swap in ⅔ cup rice) and fill half the plate with vegetables.

1. **Chicken curry + chapatis:** 150 g chicken breast or thigh (skin off) in an onion-tomato curry with 1 tsp oil, 2 chapatis and cucumber-onion salad. Batch cook enough for 2–3 nights.
2. **Fish curry + rice:** 150 g white fish (basa, barramundi or ling) in a tamarind or coconut-light curry, with ⅔ cup rice and stir-fried beans or cabbage (poriyal).
3. **Dal + paneer:** 1 cup dal (toor or moong) + 100 g paneer tikka (pan-grilled or air-fried), with 2 chapatis and sautéed spinach.
4. **Egg curry:** 3 boiled eggs in a light masala gravy, with 2 chapatis and a vegetable side.
5. **Chicken or tofu stir-fry:** 150 g chicken or firm tofu, frozen stir-fry veg, soy, garlic and chilli, with ⅔ cup rice or 2 chapatis. About 15 minutes.
6. **Kheema with peas:** 150 g lean beef or chicken mince cooked with peas, onion and spices, with 2 chapatis. Cook a batch on Sunday.
7. **Sambar + grilled chicken:** a bowl of vegetable-heavy sambar with grilled chicken pieces, plus 1 dosa or ⅔ cup rice.
8. **Salmon + roast veg:** a salmon fillet with air-fried or oven-roasted pumpkin, zucchini and capsicum. Low-carb nights are fine occasionally.

**Tips:**

- **Thicken without cream.** Use tomato, onion or a spoon of Greek yogurt instead of cream or lots of coconut milk.
- **Measure the oil.** A tadka with 1 tsp of oil or ghee is enough. Most of the extra calories hide here.
- **Make chapatis smaller and drier.** Skip ghee on top, or use wholemeal or multigrain atta.
- **Cook ahead.** Cooking a curry base or protein once makes 2–3 dinners and cuts weeknight cooking to reheating.

## Workout Plan

### Strength: 3 days a week (e.g. Mon/Wed/Fri), about 45 minutes, full body

| Exercise | Sets × Reps |
|---|---|
| Goblet squat or leg press | 3 × 8–12 |
| Dumbbell or machine chest press | 3 × 8–12 |
| Lat pulldown or seated row | 3 × 8–12 |
| Romanian deadlift (dumbbells) | 3 × 10 |
| Shoulder press | 2 × 10 |
| Plank | 3 × 30–45 s |

- **Progression:** add a little weight or one rep each week.
- **Effort:** stop each set with 1–2 reps left in the tank.

### Cardio & daily movement

- 1–2 brisk or incline walks a week, 45 minutes each.
- 7,000–8,000 steps a day. The walk after dinner covers much of the gap and helps blood sugar.

### Gym-day timing

- **Morning:** train around 6:00 am after coffee, then have breakfast straight afterwards.
- **Evening:** train around 5:00–5:45 pm after the afternoon snack, then have dinner at 6:30 pm.

## Tracking Progress

- Weigh in 3–4 mornings a week and compare weekly averages. A falling average means you're in a deficit.
- Measure your waist every 2 weeks. The scale may move slowly as you build muscle.
- Sleep 7+ hours, because recovery matters.

## Safety

If you're new to lifting, or have heart, blood pressure or joint issues, check with your GP before starting. A few sessions with a personal trainer to learn good form is worth it.

> These calorie figures are estimates. Portion sizes and cooking oil cause the biggest variation.
